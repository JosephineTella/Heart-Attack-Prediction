### Overview
Myocardial infarction (MI), commonly known as a heart attack, is a major manifestation of Coronary artery disease (CAD) and remains one of the leading causes of mortality worldwide. MI occurs when blood flow to a region of the myocardium is abruptly reduced or completely blocked, most often due to atherosclerotic plaque rupture and subsequent thrombus formation. Prolonged ischemia results in irreversible cardiomyocyte death and can lead to severe complications including heart failure, arrhythmias, and sudden cardiac death. Traditional cardiovascular risk prediction models—such as the Framingham Risk Score—rely on statistical regression methods and predefined clinical variables. Although clinically useful, these models assume linear relationships and may not adequately capture complex interactions among demographic, biochemical, lifestyle, and genetic factors. Machine learning (ML) provides a data-driven framework capable of modeling nonlinear relationships, identifying hidden patterns, and integrating high-dimensional healthcare data. The application of machine learning in cardiovascular medicine has shown promising results in early detection, risk stratification, and decision support. This project proposes the development of a machine learning–based predictive models for early identification of individuals at risk of heart attack.
